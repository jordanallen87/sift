# UX review — the pane, read as a person would read it

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Method:** `pnpm test:journey` (ADR 0014) captures a screenshot after every turn of four journeys. This review is those screenshots read as screens, plus the observations the turns recorded along the way.
**Scope:** the 430px right pane, which is the canonical surface, with 390/480/1440 checked from the E2E baselines.

**Provenance:** one model reading rendered screens. Not a user study, and not evidence that real people find this usable. What it *is* good for is catching a screen that contradicts itself, claims something untrue, or leaves a person without a next move — and it found six of those.

---

## Fixed in this pass

Each of these was visible on screen and invisible to every existing test.

### 1. The answer-first region never named the answer

A completed investigation favouring the RAV4 rendered the heading **"Current recommendation"** with the car named nowhere above the fold. `ready_blocked` — the phase that actually *has* an answer — was the only one whose headline was a section label; every other phase had a real sentence.

> **Now:** "Leading so far: 2022 Toyota RAV4 XLE Hybrid AWD", and "Sift recommends <option>." with "Your decision." when a decision is pending. Never "Our pick", because readiness is not earned in those phases (change-set §38).

### 2. The dock deleted the one action only a person can take

`MAX_DOCK_ACTIONS = 2` and a plain `slice(0, 2)`. `confirm_shortlist` is the **only** human-only move and is **sixth** in the derived order, so on any case where two earlier moves applied it never appeared — and the note "only you can take this step" went with it. The component's own header explains the cost: *"the product's central claim would be missing from the exact screen where it matters most."*

> **Now:** `selectDockActions` keeps human-only moves through truncation, without reordering what is shown. Still never more than two.

### 3. The comparison claimed priorities the person had never given

On the **first screen** a new person sees — 0 of 5 covered, nothing told to Sift — the comparison card read:

> "2022 Subaru Outback Premium AWD scores highest **against what you said matters**. …measured across 95% of **the weight you have assigned**."

The orientation shell two inches above said the honest opposite: "Sift has not asked you everything yet, so this is based on the catalog rather than on what matters to you."

> **Now:** "…scores highest **on the starting priorities**. …measured across 95% of the weight — **none of which you have set yet. Answering Sift's questions will change this.**"

### 4. Car vocabulary on an energy decision

Home Energy Guardian — a case about whether to get an HVAC inspection — offered **"Confirm your test-drive shortlist"**, explained as *"only you can decide which models are worth going to see"*, and told the person *"then you confirm which models are worth going to see."* Three pieces of copy from a different pack, on a generic surface.

> **Now:** "Confirm what moves forward" / "Only you can decide which options go ahead" / "Then you confirm which options move forward." The better long-term answer is a pack-declared term for what its options *are*; that is a pack-schema change and is flagged, not made.

### 5. "Ready for your decision" directly above "4 findings need your attention"

At **8 of 8 covered**, three lines within one screen carried two meanings: the orientation said ready, the alert said needs attention, the hero said "4 findings may need a closer look before Sift can finish". `deriveDecisionPhase` reaches `deciding` the moment a recommendation is `ready`, which is not the same as nothing being outstanding.

> **Now:** the phase label is **"Yours to decide"** — it names whose turn it is, which is true either way, and leaves the readiness claim to the hero, which knows. Fixing the phase derivation instead would mean a second implementation of "flagged findings", and two derivations that agree today are two that can drift.

### 6. The pane named an assistant it could not detect

Covered in ADR 0013: "WebMCP ready — ChatGPT can operate this page directly", rendered for the first time by Chrome. A second instance — the dock's "Sift and ChatGPT can explain it" — was found here and fixed the same way.

## Observed, not changed — for your call

### A. Sift answers before it asks

At 0 of 5 covered, the pane already shows a ranked comparison and a leader. It is now honest about that (finding 3), and there is a real argument for it: the product is useful in the first fifteen seconds, which is the hero demo's whole opening. But the first thing a new person reads is still an answer to a question nobody asked them.

**Options, in order of how much I'd recommend them:** leave it and rely on the now-honest framing; or collapse the comparison card until one topic is confirmed, so the first screen is a question rather than a verdict. The second is a real product decision about the demo's opening, which is why I did not make it.

### B. "Review findings" appears twice, adjacent

The alert banner and the hero both render a "Review findings" button, roughly 200px apart, pointing at the same sheet. The banner is derived from the same `flaggedFindingsCount` the hero is. One of the two is redundant; I'd drop it from the banner and leave the banner as a statement, since the hero is the region a person is already reading.

### C. The "LATEST COMMAND" block is developer content on a consumer surface

It renders `… → Completed → In progress → Waiting for confirmation → Completed` — an internal state machine — directly under the recommendation. ADR 0004 already moved the activity ledger to the Runtime Inspector for exactly this reason. This block appears to have survived that move.

### D. "Sift is looking into 10 things across 1 option"

Correct (deep work only, by design) but it reads oddly next to "4 options" in the app bar. "across 1 of your 4 options" would remove the double-take.

### E. The "WHAT TO WATCH FOR" tint reads as an error

A pink/red-tinted block whose contents are `still needs stronger evidence` ×3 — a normal in-progress state — sits next to genuinely neutral sections. The same tint elsewhere means a real problem.

## What holds up well

Worth saying, because a review that only lists problems misrepresents the product.

- **The Quick Pick affordance is excellent.** "Keep keeps it for a closer look. Nothing is decided here, and you can change any of these later." removes exactly the anxiety that makes people avoid triage UI.
- **"You said: Running and ownership cost"** — reflecting a person's own words back is the cheapest trust-building move available, and it is done well.
- **The unknowns are genuinely unknown.** "STILL RESEARCHING / is still unknown" for three items, rather than a fabricated number, is the product's central claim rendered as ordinary copy.
- **The recommendation's LIMITATIONS section argues against itself** ("The favored candidate's deal terms are still under review… The reasoning above may account for something the scoring does not — it is worth reading before deciding"). Very few products do this.
- **The narrow pane holds at 390px** with no overflow, no overlap, and a legible hierarchy, including with a two-line headline.

---

## What this review cannot tell you

- Whether real people can complete the journey. Nobody was watched using it.
- Whether the tool descriptions read well **to a model**. `pnpm webmcp:bridge` exists for that: point Codex or Claude Code at it and watch what it chooses unprompted.
- Anything about screen readers beyond the automated axe passes already in the E2E suite.
